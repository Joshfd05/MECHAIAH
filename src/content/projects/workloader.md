problem: |-
  Workloader was made for someone whose week does not fit in one notebook. Study, project deadlines, and things promised to other people were living in chats, scraps of paper, and memory. A plan written on Sunday was already wrong by Wednesday, and nothing sent a reminder when a date slipped.

  The planner had to do four things. Show what today is for. Keep related work together, such as one subject, one project, or one deadline. Put those dates on a calendar. Nudge the person before something is due. It also had to stay private to the person who signs in, and it had to be usable on a phone during the day and on a larger screen when planning the week.
built: |-
  Workloader is a personal planner. You sign in with Google and get your own space. The home screen greets you by name and tells you how much of today is still open. From there you can add a task or an event in a few seconds, set a date, and break a task into steps.

  Work that belongs together sits in a project. Each project shows how far along it is, what is still open, and what is overdue. Opening a project shows that work on its own, so a busy week does not turn into one long list. The calendar shows the same tasks and events by month, week, or day, including things that repeat, such as a weekly class. Reminders fire before a task or a deadline, including a notice on the device, so the plan is not something you have to remember to open.

  The plan was shaped around a real week, not a wishlist. The first question was what a person needs to see on a Monday morning: what is due, what can wait, and what needs a nudge. Screens followed that order. Today comes first. Projects hold the longer pieces of work. The calendar holds the dates. When a week goes off track, dates can be moved and the rest of the plan shifts with them, instead of starting the list again. Each account stays separate, so one person's deadlines are not mixed with another's.

  Someone using it can open the app and know what to do next, see which project is falling behind, and trust that a deadline will show up before the day arrives.
